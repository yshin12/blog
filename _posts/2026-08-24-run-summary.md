### Notes

This was a textbook negative-split shape: every kilometre from 1 through 8 was faster than the one
before it, a 60-second swing from 5:05 to 4:05 /km (8:11 to 6:34 /mi) with no lurching. Km 6–9
averaged about 4:10 /km (6:42 /mi), which lands right on the LT band of 4:03–4:09 /km
(6:31–6:41 /mi) — call it roughly 17 minutes of threshold work embedded in an easy run.

Cadence tracked the effort cleanly, climbing from 173 spm in the opening kilometre to 190–192 spm
through the fast section, and stride length went from 112 cm to 128 cm. That is the right way to
find speed: turnover and stride opening together rather than overstriding. Heart rate topped out at
162 average / 167 max in km 9, around 87% of estimated max — genuine threshold, not over the line
into VO2max.

**Plan adherence:** Monday in the 6-weeks-to-goal week is prescribed as rest or cross-training, with
a 6–8 km recovery jog as the accepted alternative. Ten kilometres finishing at LT pace is a good
deal more than that. It is not a problem in isolation, but Wednesday holds the VO2max session
(3 × 1,200 m + 3 × 1,000 m), so Tuesday's 14 km general aerobic should be run genuinely easy —
5:00–5:40 /km (8:03–9:07 /mi), heart rate under about 143 — to protect that workout.

On the left hamstring/glute: threshold pace is exactly the intensity to watch, and the data looks
reassuring — no cadence drop or stride shortening across the fast kilometres, and the cooldown
kilometre came back smoothly at 4:49 /km. Worth a check on the sit-bone area first thing tomorrow;
if anything registers at 2/10 or above, trim Tuesday and keep Wednesday's intervals intact.
