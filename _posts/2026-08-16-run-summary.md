A **3:18 negative split** over matched halves. The kilometre-by-kilometre ladder runs 5:34, 5:14,
5:07, 5:05, 5:06, 4:59, 4:57, 4:53, 4:55, 4:49, 4:48, 4:48, 4:45, 4:46, 4:40, 4:41, 4:40, 4:35 —
three one-second blips in eighteen splits, and the fastest kilometre is the last one. That is
exactly what Pfitzinger means by "progressing faster through the run," and it is a discipline most
runners fail in the opposite direction, going out at goal effort and hanging on.

The opening kilometre at 5:34 /km (8:57 /mi) with HR at 112 deserves its own mention. Starting an
endurance run slower than recovery pace is the hardest easy thing to do, and it is what bought the
last six kilometres.

### Heart rate and mechanics

Average HR of 142 bpm sits at roughly 81% of max — the ceiling of the general aerobic band — and
the run finished at 161 bpm (about 91%), which is threshold territory. So this was an endurance run
that ended as a genuine tempo effort. Pace tells the same story: the 4:54 /km (7:53 /mi) average
lands at the fast end of the endurance band (4:52–5:24 /km, 7:50–8:41 /mi), while the closing
kilometres at 4:40–4:35 /km (7:31–7:22 /mi) sit between endurance and the LT band of
4:03–4:09 /km (6:31–6:41 /mi).

Cadence climbed cleanly with the pace — 170 spm on the first kilometre to 188–190 spm over the last
four — and stride length went 1.04 m to 1.15 m. Turnover and extension rose together, which is the
signature of a body that is fresh and accelerating, not one that is grinding. There is no late-run
cadence collapse anywhere in the file.

### Context

Week 8-to-goal was the recovery week, target 75 km (46.6 mi). Today closes it at
**97.0 km (60.3 mi)** — about 22 km (14 mi) over, and within a few kilometres of the planned peak
week that is still four weeks away. Yesterday's note suggested trimming today to 14–16 km at the
easy end of the band; the run went the full 18 km and finished near threshold instead. It was a
good run, and the week is now a hard week wearing a recovery week's label.

That matters because week 7-to-goal starts tomorrow with real work in it: Wednesday is
lactate threshold, 16 km (10 mi) with a 22-minute and an 18-minute LT interval, and Sunday is a
23 km (14 mi) progression long run closing with 5 km at LT pace. The 89 km (55 mi) target assumes
the legs arrive rested. Monday is "rest or cross-training" on the card — after a 97 km week ending
in a tempo-flavoured 18 km, take the full rest day, or cap it at 6–8 km (4–5 mi) genuinely easy at
5:25–5:55 /km (8:45–9:30 /mi).

For the left hamstring/glute, this was the biggest single load it has carried since the niggle
appeared, and the data reads clean: no pace fade, no cadence drop, no shortening of stride in the
closing kilometres — nothing that looks like guarding. The rolling terrain was mild (48 m gain) and
there were no hills or sharp accelerations to spike the tendon. Tomorrow morning is the test that
counts. Quiet sit-bone on waking and Wednesday's threshold block stays as written; stiffness or
tenderness at 2/10 or more and the LT minutes come down first while the endurance volume stays.
Isometric holds over stretching, as always.
