### Classification and plan adherence

- **Prescribed (Pfitz transition week, Sun):** endurance run, 18 km.
- **Actual:** 10.45 km of easy-to-general-aerobic running that got faster each kilometre, from 5:59 down to 5:11 /km, with HR going from 112 to 133. Three strides followed, for 11.2 km in total.
- The run was shorter and easier than the 18 km on the plan, and a bit under the 12–14 km suggested after yesterday's run. That is reasonable two weeks after a half marathon and right after four days off. The strides were extra, since none were planned for today.

### What went well

- **Clean negative split.** Every kilometre from km 2 to km 10 was a little faster, and the 5:11 tenth kilometre was the fastest of the day. This was the right way to run it: easy start, controlled finish.
- **Low HR for the pace.** HR was 129–133 bpm at 5:11–5:28 /km, at the low end of the GA zone (123–143). It matches yesterday's numbers, so two runs in a row show that fitness held through the rest block.
- **Stride length grew as pace rose.** Stride length went from 0.97 m to 1.07 m while cadence held at 180–185 spm. A longer stride late in the run is a good sign for the left hamstring/glute, because no shortening or guarding showed up.

### To watch

- **Strides were still quick.** They ran at about 18–19 s per 100 m against the 20–22 s target, the same as yesterday. Keep them at a smooth 90% effort, because the groin strain began with near-sprint strides.
- **Recoveries were too short.** HR climbed from 136 to 157 to 166 bpm across only three strides. Take 60–90 s of walking or jogging between strides so HR falls below ~130 each time.
- **Slow first 3 km.** Cadence was 172–178 spm and stride length was down in km 1–3. A 5-minute walk or some leg swings before starting would help you warm up so the run doesn't have to do it.
