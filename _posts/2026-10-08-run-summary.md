## Recovery run: 5.26 mi (8.47 km)

This was the last real run before Sunday's half marathon, three days out. It was easy and controlled: the distance matched the 5 mi prescription, HR stayed in the low-to-mid 120s, and cadence rose through the run. For the second day running, HR at easy pace looked the same as before the flu.

### Plan adherence

- **Prescribed (post-flu race-week plan):** 5 mi (8 km) recovery at 8:45–9:30 /mi (5:25–5:55 /km), HR < 133. The strides were dropped to protect the left groin.
- **Actual:** 5.26 mi at 8:41 /mi, average HR 126, no strides. **On plan.** The average pace was 4 s/mi faster than the band, which doesn't matter in cool, dry air.

### Notes

- **Good:** HR averaged 126 (72% of max) and moved only from 122 to 128 across the full miles. Cadence climbed from 172 to 182 while stride length held at 1.02–1.04 m. Those are fresh, relaxed legs, which is what a taper is for.
- **Watch:** mile 5 at 8:25 /mi (5:14 /km) and the last stretch at 8:17 /mi (5:09 /km) were quicker than the recovery band. This close to the race, don't chase the feeling of running fast. Saturday's shakeout should feel almost too slow.
- **Hamstring/glute and groin:** stride length was even from start to finish, with no sign of guarding on the left side. Today was the last day of isometric bridge holds. Do no strength work and no hamstring stretching until after the race.

### What's next

- **Fri Oct 9:** rest, and carb-load day 1 (600–750 g carbs, low fiber, plenty of fluids while travelling).
- **Sat Oct 10:** shakeout of 3–4 mi (5–6 km) at 8:45–9:30 /mi (5:25–5:55 /km). Add 4 relaxed strides only if the groin is at 0/10.
- **Sun Oct 11: half marathon, 13.1 mi (21.1 km).** Run miles 1–3 at 6:55 /mi (4:18 /km). If HR is ≤ 160 at 5 km, settle into 6:49 /mi (4:14 /km), which is about 1:29:20.
