This was the muggiest run of the week. The temperature itself, 22.8 °C (73 °F),
is only a modest tax, but the **21.1 °C (70 °F) dew point at 89 % humidity** is
the part that matters — that is sticky air with almost no evaporative cooling
available. The temp+dew sum of **143 °F** lands in the 140–149 band, which is
worth roughly **3–4.5 % slower** for the same effort. So the nominal general
aerobic band of 5:00–5:40 /km (8:03–9:07 /mi) shifts to a heat-equivalent
**5:09–5:55 /km (8:18–9:31 /mi)** today. Running 5:14 /km (8:25 /mi) at 132 bpm
in that air is the cool-weather equivalent of about **5:03 /km (8:08 /mi)** —
a genuinely strong aerobic day disguised as an ordinary one.

### Splits and shape

A textbook aerobic progression, run entirely on feel. The opening kilometre was a
proper warm-up at 5:36 /km (9:01 /mi) with HR at just 114 bpm, then the pace
settled and quietly rolled downhill: **first 7 km averaged 5:19 /km (8:33 /mi)**,
**last 7 km averaged 5:10 /km (8:19 /mi)** — a 9 s/km negative split with no
kilometre slower than 5:19 after the opener.

The pacing discipline in the back half is the standout. Kilometres 8 through 12
came in at 5:08, 5:07, 5:08, 5:08, 5:08 — five consecutive splits inside one
second of each other. That is metronomic.

HR tells the honest story of the humidity: 114 bpm on the opening kilometre,
into the low 130s by km 5, topping out at 139 bpm around km 8, then **settling
back to 133–135 bpm for the final four kilometres despite holding the faster
pace.** Average 132 bpm sits mid-window in the 70–81 % max HR general aerobic
band (123–143 bpm), and the fact that HR flattened rather than drifted upward
over the last third — in 89 % humidity — is a clean fitness signal.

Cadence held 181 spm average and peaked at 185–186 spm through the fast middle
block, with stride length opening from 1.02 m to a maximum of 1.08 m at km 12.
Getting faster by lengthening stride while cadence stays high is the efficient
way to do it. Terrain was essentially flat (32 m gain, 33 m loss).

### Plan adherence

Week 4 to goal (Sep 7 – Sep 13) prescribes for Wednesday: **general aerobic,
14 km (9 mi).** Actual: **14.03 km (8.72 mi)** at 5:14 /km, HR 132 bpm.

That is a bullseye — distance exactly on target, intensity squarely in the
general aerobic HR window, and no hidden intensity creeping in. With the
**8K–10K tune-up race on Saturday Sep 12** three days out, holding this to a
true aerobic effort rather than letting the good legs push it toward tempo was

puts the week at 30.5 km (19.0 mi) through Wednesday, on track for the 84 km
target.

### Notes

No hamstring or glute warning signs in the data. Stride length **increased**
through the run rather than shortening, cadence stayed even, and the last four
kilometres were the fastest and steadiest of the day — none of which happens
when a proximal hamstring is guarding. Flat terrain and zero hills also meant no
provocative loading in the sit-bone region.

With the tune-up race on Saturday, the next two days matter more than usual:
Thursday is a **recovery 11 km (7 mi)** and Friday is rest or very easy. Keep
Thursday genuinely slow — 5:25–5:55 /km (8:45–9:30 /mi) — and check the sit-bone
area on Friday morning before committing to a hard race effort. If the humidity
holds through Saturday, plan an earlier start and carry water; the race-day
target should be adjusted for heat rather than chased on paper.
