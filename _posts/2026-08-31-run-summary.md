Two activities in Burlington this evening: a 20.43 km progression endurance run starting at
18:07, then a short 2.05 km recovery jog at 19:50. Total 22.48 km (13.97 mi) in 1:50:03 of
running. This was Sunday's endurance session shifted one day onto Monday — the biggest single
run of the block so far, and executed as a textbook progression.

**Run type: Endurance / progression long run (LR).** The Pfitzinger endurance band is
4:52–5:24 /km (7:50–8:41 /mi) with effort building through the run. This one started at the
easy end and finished well past it — the closing 10 km averaged about 4:38 /km (7:27 /mi),
faster than endurance pace but still clearly short of LT (4:03–4:09 /km). Call it a strong
steady-state finish rather than a threshold workout.

Three clean phases. Kilometres 1–3 were a genuine warm-up, easing from 5:20 to 4:57 with HR
still only in the 130s. Kilometres 4–11 locked onto 4:47–4:51 /km and held it almost without
variance — eight consecutive splits inside a five-second window. From kilometre 12 the pace
stepped down again to 4:36–4:42 /km and stayed there to the finish.

The cadence trace tells the same story: 166 spm on the opening kilometre, 175–178 through the
steady block, then 182–189 over the final nine kilometres. Turnover increased with effort
while stride length actually shortened slightly (1.19 m → 1.15 m), which means the speed came
from leg speed rather than reaching — the mechanically safer way to progress.

Heart rate drifted from 114 to 164 bpm across the run. Roughly half of that is genuine
progression and half is cardiac drift over 97 minutes, which is normal for a run of this
length. Peak HR of 167 bpm arrived only in the last kilometre.

6:01, HR settling at 135 bpm and never exceeding 141. Short stride (0.98 m) and reduced cadence
confirm this was a shakeout, not a second workout.

**Sequencing.** The main run finished around 19:45 and the jog began at 19:50 — a five-minute
gap, so the second activity is functionally a cool-down that happened to be recorded separately.
That is good practice after finishing a long run at 4:38 /km with HR at 164: twelve minutes of
easy jogging to bring the heart rate down gradually rather than stopping cold.

**Load.** High. Nearly two hours of running and 1,686 kcal make this the largest single day of
the block. The last hour was spent at 150–164 bpm, so the aerobic stimulus was substantial —
this run should be treated as a key session, and the days around it adjusted accordingly.

### Plan note

Monday in Pfitzinger week 5 (Aug 31 – Sep 6, 97 km) reads "rest or cross-training", and Sunday
of week 6 called for a 24 km (14.9 mi) endurance run that was not recorded. So today is the
Sunday long run taken one day late — a clean adjacent-day swap, and 20.43 km against a
prescribed 24 km is a fair account of it. Week 6 closed short of its 92 km target, but the
endurance piece that anchors the week did get done.

The progression shape is the encouraging part. Building from 5:20 to 4:38 /km over 20 km, with
cadence rising and stride length holding, is precisely what the plan asks of a progression run,
and it was achieved without straying into threshold territory.

Two things follow. First, with Monday's rest day now spent, the week is effectively pushed back
a day — Tuesday's 14 km general aerobic is better run genuinely easy, at 5:00–5:40 /km
(8:03–9:07 /mi), or trimmed, and Wednesday's 40-minute tempo is the session to protect. Second,
the left hamstring/glute niggle deserves attention here: 97 minutes of running with a
progressively faster finish is the highest tissue load it has seen since the niggle appeared.
Nothing in the data suggests trouble — cadence rose and stride length stayed even to the last
kilometre, with no late-run pace collapse or asymmetry in the splits — but the honest test is
tomorrow morning. If the sit-bone area is quiet, carry on. If it registers 2/10 or more, keep
Tuesday easy and move Wednesday's tempo later in the week, favouring isometric holds over
stretching.
