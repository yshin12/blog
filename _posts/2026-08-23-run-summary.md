One activity today: a long, steady Sunday run of 19.41 km held firmly in the easy aerobic band. The
plan called for a 23 km progression long run finishing with 5 km at LT pace; what actually happened
was a flat, relaxed 1:52 with the heart rate parked in the 120s and 130s the whole way. Shorter and
much softer than prescribed — but as a clean aerobic block closing out an 89 km plan week, it did
its job without adding any stress to the left hamstring.

### Notes

Pacing was remarkably flat: from km 2 onward every split sat between 5:34 and 5:53 /km
(8:58–9:28 /mi), a spread of under 20 seconds across 18 km. Cadence held at 180–183 spm almost
throughout, only dipping slightly on the rolling section around km 19, and stride length stayed
near 95 cm — no mechanical decay over nearly two hours on feet.

Heart rate drift was modest and well controlled. The first half averaged around 126 bpm, the second
half around 136 bpm, peaking at 147 in the km 13–15 stretch before settling again. Average of
131 bpm is roughly 77% of estimated max — recovery to low general-aerobic territory. The last
0.41 km came in at 5:01 /km (8:04 /mi), a small natural pickup rather than a real LT finish.

**Plan adherence:** prescribed was a progression long run of 23 km (14 mi) with the last 5 km at LT
pace (4:03–4:09 /km, 6:31–6:41 /mi). Actual was 19.41 km at a flat 5:46 /km with no threshold work
— about 3.6 km short and considerably easier. Given the left hamstring/glute niggle, skipping the LT
finish is the right order of priority: endurance volume first, threshold load second. The pace was
also slower than the endurance band (4:52–5:24 /km), so this ran closer to a recovery long run.

Nothing in the data suggests hamstring irritation — cadence stable, stride length even, no late-run
pace collapse. Worth checking how the sit-bone area feels tomorrow morning before deciding whether
Tuesday's 14 km general aerobic goes as written.
