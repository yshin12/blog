against a cadence of 170 spm is the signature of a deliberate shuffle — short steps, quick
feet, minimal ground force. That is exactly how a pre-quality shakeout should look.

### Structure

**Warm-up (km 1–3):** 5:47, 5:23, 5:15 /km (9:18, 8:39, 8:27 /mi) — a proper progressive
warm-up settling into the general aerobic band of 4:52–5:17/km (7:50–8:30/mi) by the third
kilometre.

**Hill repetitions (km 4–5):** 56 m (184 ft) of climbing in the fourth kilometre and another
34 m (112 ft) in the fifth, with best paces inside those splits of 3:20/km and 3:39/km
(5:21 and 5:52/mi) and cadence spiking to 214 spm. Heart rate peaked at 163 bpm here — the
high point of the day. This is the 6 × 12-second uphill block, and the cadence spike
confirms it was run as short, fast, powerful efforts rather than a grind.

**Aerobic block (km 6–9):** 5:44, 5:27, 5:39, 5:44 /km (9:14, 8:46, 9:05, 9:13 /mi) with HR
settling back from 142 to 131. Sensible: absorb the hills, get the heart rate down, then hit
the strides fresh.

**Strides (8 × ~100 m):** eight repetitions of 20–22 seconds each, averaging 3:36–3:46/km
(5:47–6:04/mi) across the whole lap and touching 2:59–3:12/km (4:48–5:09/mi) at peak speed,
with roughly 55–60 seconds of jogging between them. The 20–22 second duration is precisely
the Pfitzinger 100 m stride specification. Lap averages land just outside the 3:20–3:35/km
speed band, but that is an artefact of the acceleration and deceleration being included in
the lap — the peak velocities are comfortably faster than target.

## Plan adherence

The Pfitzinger week (10 weeks to the 2026-10-11 half) prescribes for Saturday:
**general aerobic + speed, 11 km (7 miles), 6 × 12 sec uphill followed by 8 × 100 m
strides.**

The evening run delivered 11.26 km (7.00 mi), a hill block, and eight strides. That is an
essentially perfect execution of the session — the distance is within 260 m of prescription
and both speed elements were completed in the prescribed order. The morning shakeout is an
extra on top, not a deviation.

## Hamstring / glute note

This was the highest-risk session of the week for the minor left hamstring/glute niggle:
hill sprints and strides load the proximal hamstring tendon harder than anything else on the
schedule. Two things worked in its favour. First, the hard work was uphill — uphill sprinting
involves less hip flexion at speed and a shorter stride than flat sprinting, so the proximal
tendon sees less tensile-plus-compressive load. Second, the strides came after nine
kilometres of running rather than early, so the tissue was thoroughly warm.

The data shows no distress: cadence during the aerobic block stayed even at 175–181 spm, the
final strides were the fastest of the set rather than the slowest, and there is no
deterioration in the closing kilometres. Nothing suggests the runner was protecting the leg.

Tomorrow morning is the real test. If the sit-bone area reads 2/10 or above, or feels stiff
on getting out of bed, trim Sunday's endurance run and keep it flat. Keep loading isometric —
long-hold bridges, single-leg isometric holds — rather than stretching the hamstring, since
stretching compresses the proximal tendon.

The sequencing was well judged. A 10-hour, 20-minute gap between the two runs is ample
recovery, and the split of work — 5 km of pure easy volume in the morning, all of the
intensity concentrated in one evening session — means the hard work happened on legs that
were loosened but not fatigued. The alternative, tacking the extra 5 km onto the front of the
evening run, would have put the hill reps and strides on tireder legs for no extra benefit.

Cardiovascular load for the day was modest despite the double: a weighted average of ~126 bpm
across 97 minutes, with only the two hill kilometres pushing into the 150s and 160s. The
1,239 calories are gross figures and include resting metabolism during the runs.

### Takeaway

A textbook execution of the hardest session type to get right. The hills were run as genuine
short sprints, the strides hit the correct duration with the fastest reps last, and the
morning shakeout did its job without adding meaningful fatigue. The only thing to manage is
the arithmetic: the week is running hot on volume, and Sunday is the place to give some back.
