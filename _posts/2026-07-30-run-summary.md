A short, deliberately gentle shakeout the day after a hard session.

**Run type: Recovery (AR).** Everything about this one says active recovery rather than easy-aerobic:
average HR of 122 bpm is roughly 71% of age-predicted max and never climbed above 133, and the
average pace of 5:48/km sits well outside normal easy range (5:00–5:30/km). Under 40 minutes and
under 7 km, it is a flush-out rather than a training stimulus — exactly the right call.

### Split pattern

The kilometre splits were quietly progressive: 6:07, 5:50, 5:45, 5:45, 5:41, 5:41, then 5:46 for
the closing 0.43 km. That is a 26-second drop from km 1 to km 6 with HR drifting only from 110 to
127 — a clean warm-up curve where the body opens up on its own rather than being pushed. Cadence
followed the same shape, climbing from 171 to a stable 177–179 spm and holding there.

### Context

This follows a genuinely hard Wednesday (14.09 km at 4:48/km, avg HR 151, max HR 170, best pace
dipping to 2:52/km — a clear interval/threshold session) and a 14.01 km moderate effort on Tuesday.
Backing off to a sub-7 km jog at 5:48/km after that block is textbook sequencing, and it matters
more than usual given the minor left hamstring/glute pain reported yesterday.

Nothing in the data suggests that niggle was aggravated: stride length dropped to 96.8 cm from
113.1 cm yesterday, cadence stayed high and even, and there was no split-to-split degradation or
asymmetric slow-down that would hint at guarding. Short stride plus high cadence is a
tendon-friendly combination — it keeps hip-flexion angles modest and limits tensile load on the
proximal hamstring.

### Takeaway

A disciplined recovery day. Keep loading isometric (long-hold bridges, single-leg isometric holds)
rather than stretching the hamstring, and if the sit-bone area reaches 2/10 or is stiff tomorrow
morning, keep the next session easy and flat before returning to any tempo or hill work.
