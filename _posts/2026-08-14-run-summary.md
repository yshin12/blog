The shape is close to ideal for an easy aerobic run. After a 5:33 opening kilometre the pace
settled into a 5:18–5:25 corridor, dipped to 5:10–5:13 through kilometres 9 and 10, then closed
right where it started. Total spread across kilometres 2–15 was just 17 s/km (27 s/mi).

The heart rate story is the better one. Average 125 bpm — roughly 70% of max — with a peak of only
135, against a pace within a few seconds of general aerobic. First-half average was about 122 bpm,
second-half about 127: a 5 bpm drift across 78 minutes while *gaining* a little speed. That is a
well-fuelled, well-hydrated run and a meaningful marker two weeks into training after clearance.
For comparison, Wednesday's session ran 5:01 /km (8:04 /mi) at 137 bpm; today traded 20 s/km for
12 bpm.

Mechanics held up too. Cadence sat at 175 spm from the first kilometre to the last, and stride
length grew from 1.03 m early to 1.12 m at the quick patch before settling at 1.07 m. The extra
speed in the middle came from a longer, freer stride rather than a faster shuffle — a relaxed
runner, not a labouring one.

### Context

Week 8-to-goal is the recovery week with a 75 km (47 mi) target, and today puts the week at
**68.9 km (42.8 mi) through Friday** with Saturday and Sunday still on the card. Running both
weekend sessions at full prescribed distance would land near 101 km (63 mi) — above the planned
peak week, in a week whose job is absorption. The sensible move is to trim the weekend rather than
chase the total, especially with next Wednesday's big threshold block waiting: 16 km with 22 min
and 18 min at LT pace, 4:03–4:09 /km (6:31–6:41 /mi).

The one item still owed is speed. Thursday's strides were dropped, and today had no fast segments
at all — best pace of 4:44 /km (7:37 /mi) shows no stride signature. Saturday is already a general
aerobic plus speed day, so the 150 m repeats can land there and keep the neuromuscular thread
alive.

For the left hamstring/glute, this was a low-risk session: easy pace, only 44 m of climbing, no
accelerations. Nothing in the data suggests guarding — steady cadence and a stride that lengthened
rather than shortened late are both reassuring signs. Saturday's strides will be the first real
test of that tendon this week, so building into each rep over the first 40–50 m on flat ground is
the way in.
