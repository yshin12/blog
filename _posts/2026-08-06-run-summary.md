Eleven consecutive miles, every one faster than the last except the final partial — a textbook
monotonic negative split. The run opened at recovery pace (5:27/km) and closed at 4:31/km,
a 56 s/km swing from first mile to last. The first half averaged roughly 5:12/km (8:22/mi)
and the second half 4:39/km (7:29/mi).

Heart rate tracked the effort honestly: 116 bpm at the start, 159 at the finish, averaging
139 — around 74% of max, with the peak of 161 touching only ~86%. For 18 km on a warm
afternoon, that is a genuinely aerobic run despite the closing speed.

### Plan adherence

Week 9 of the Pfitzinger build prescribed **Thursday: endurance, 18 km (11 mi)**, target band
4:39–5:05/km (7:30–8:10/mi), progressing faster through the run.

| | Prescribed | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| Distance | 18 km (11 mi) | 18.12 km (11.26 mi) |
| Type | Endurance, progressive | Progression, negative split |
| Avg pace | 4:39–5:05 /km | 4:54 /km |

Distance to the metre, average pace mid-band, and the "progress through the run" instruction
followed more literally than most people ever manage. The opening two miles sat slower than
the endurance band and the closing three sat faster — which is precisely what a progression
run is supposed to look like, not a deviation.

One honest note: yesterday's read was that the slower end of the band was the smarter play
after 37 minutes of threshold work. Today went the other way and finished at 4:31/km. The HR
data says it was absorbed comfortably, so this is an observation rather than a correction —
but four consecutive days of running with a hard tempo in them is a real load, and the closing
miles were nearer to marathon effort than to easy.

### Hamstring / glute note

The numbers look reassuring. Cadence climbed steadily from 165 to 185 spm while stride length
*grew* from 102 cm to 119 cm — a guarding hamstring shows the opposite signature, stride
shortening as cadence rises to compensate. Lengthening stride under increasing pace, late in
an 18 km run, is the pattern of a tendon that is tolerating load rather than protecting itself.

Yesterday's threshold session plus today's 18 km is a substantial two-day block on a niggle
that is only a week old. Friday is a scheduled rest day, which is well placed. Keep the
isometric holds going and keep avoiding static hamstring stretching.

### Heat note

26°C again, 1,286 calories in 89 minutes. Rehydrate with sodium tonight and get carbohydrate
in early — Saturday's session has strides and hill repeats in it, and turning up flat for
those is how form breaks down.

### What's next

**Friday: rest.** Then Saturday is general aerobic + speed, 13 km (8 mi) with 6 × 12 s uphill
and 8 × 100 m strides at roughly 3:20–3:35/km (5:22–5:46/mi), 20–22 s per stride. If the
sit-bone area reads 2/10 or worse on waking, drop the uphill reps and keep the strides on the
flat.

### Takeaway

The plan asked for 18 km run progressively, and got 18.12 km with eleven straight descending
mile splits, an average squarely in the endurance band, and heart rate never leaving aerobic
territory. Executed about as well as this session can be.
