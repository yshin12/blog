### Hamstring / glute note

This was the week's riskiest session for the niggle — hill sprints and strides load the
proximal hamstring harder than anything except a full tempo — so the data is worth reading
closely, and it reads well. Hill sprint stride length did not shorten across the six reps
(148 cm on rep 1, 148 cm on rep 6), and there was no drop-off in cadence. A guarding hamstring
protects itself by cutting stride and spinning faster; that did not happen.

The one thing to watch: stride length across the eight strides fell from 177 cm to 137 cm
while times stayed flat, meaning the later reps were held together with cadence rather than
extension. That is most likely ordinary neuromuscular fatigue at the end of a session, but
reduced hip extension is also how an irritated proximal tendon presents. The morning check
matters more than usual tomorrow.

If the sit-bone area reads 2/10 or worse on waking, cut the LT finish off tomorrow's long run
and keep it purely aerobic. Keep the isometric holds going; keep avoiding static hamstring
stretching, which compresses the tendon exactly where it is sore.

### What's next

**Sunday: progression long run, 23 km (14 mi)** — build effort steadily throughout, with the
last 3 km (2 mi) at LT pace, 4:05–4:12/km (6:34–6:45/mi). Open no faster than 5:20/km
(8:35/mi); there is a lot of runway and the temptation after a sharp Saturday is to start too
quick. Fuel and hydrate properly beforehand — this is the longest run of the block so far and
it closes the 84 km week.

Hold the LT finish only if the hamstring is quiet on waking. Aerobic-only for 23 km is a
perfectly good week-closer if it is not.

### Takeaway

Twelve short, fast efforts executed inside their target windows, wrapped in 10 km of properly
paced aerobic running, with no mechanical evidence of the hamstring complaining. Textbook
adherence, and good sharpening work nine weeks out.
