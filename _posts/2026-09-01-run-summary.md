**Run type: General aerobic (AR) + strides.** The Pfitzinger general aerobic band is defined by
heart rate — 70–81% of max, roughly 123–143 bpm — with a descriptive pace window of
5:00–5:40 /km (8:03–9:07 /mi). Average HR of 132 bpm sits almost exactly mid-band, and every
one of the fourteen full kilometres landed inside the pace window. This is what the session is
supposed to look like.

A gentle, unforced progression: 5:39 /km on the opening kilometre easing down to 5:04–5:10 /km
over the second half, a total drift of about 30 seconds per kilometre spread evenly across the
run. Heart rate followed the same shape — 110 bpm at the start, settling into the high 130s and
low 140s by kilometre 8 and staying there. The last four kilometres held 140–143 bpm without
climbing further, which means the effort found a genuine steady state rather than creeping.

Stride length grew from 1.04 m to 1.11 m while cadence stayed flat at 172–175 spm, so the
gradual acceleration came from opening up rather than turning over faster. Cadence is a little
below the usual 180 spm, which is normal at this easy a pace and not worth chasing on a
recovery-oriented day.

## Plan note

Tuesday of Pfitzinger week 5 (Aug 31 – Sep 6, 97 km target) prescribes **general aerobic —
9 miles (14 km)**. Actual: 14.47 km at 5:17 /km with average HR mid-band. Full adherence, with
two unprescribed strides added on the end — a small bonus that costs nothing and keeps the legs
sharp ahead of Wednesday's tempo.

The context matters more than the numbers. Yesterday was 22.48 km across two runs, including a
20.43 km progression finishing at 4:38 /km. Coming back the next day and running 14.5 km at
5:17 /km with HR averaging 132 is a good recovery signal: the aerobic system absorbed a big day
and came back willing. Had the same pace cost 145 bpm, that would have been the flag to soften
the rest of the week.

Wednesday is the week's anchor: **lactate threshold, 16 km with a 40-minute tempo** at
4:03–4:09 /km (6:31–6:41 /mi). Today's run leaves that entirely intact — easy enough to be
genuine recovery, with enough volume to keep the week on its 97 km trajectory. Warm up
thoroughly, and treat the 40 minutes as a controlled one-hour-race effort rather than a time
trial.

On the left hamstring/glute, this was about as safe a session as the schedule offers: no hills
to speak of (+34 m over 14.5 km), steady easy pace, and even splits with no late-run
deterioration. The two strides are the only real tissue load, and short strides are generally
well tolerated by that region — the risk sits in sustained fast running, which is tomorrow's
question, not today's. If the sit-bone area is quiet in the morning, run the tempo as written.
If it registers 2/10 or more, cut the tempo to two 15-minute blocks with a jog between, and
keep to isometric holds rather than stretching the area.
