Sunday brought a long progression run in Burlington at 14:37, followed by a short
cool-down jog at 17:10. The plan called for an easy-to-moderate 19 km (11.8 mi)
endurance run. Instead you ran 26.3 km (16.3 mi) and finished with 5 km at
threshold pace, in the heat of the afternoon.

**Conditions:** 27.2 °C (81 °F), feels like 27.2 °C (81 °F), dew point 16.1 °C
(61 °F), 51 % humidity, wind 11 km/h (7 mph) from the W, Burlington Piers. For the
cool-down it was the same temperature with a dew point of 13.9 °C (57 °F) and 45 %
humidity. Temperature plus dew point was 142 °F, which usually costs about
3–4.5 % in pace. That turns the threshold band of 4:03–4:09 /km (6:31–6:41 /mi) into
a heat-adjusted **4:11–4:20 /km (6:44–6:58 /mi)**. Expect HR to run above its usual
zone in these conditions.

You held back well early and got faster almost every kilometre after that, from
6:44 /km (10:50 /mi) to 4:28 /km (7:09 /mi) by km 20. Then you ran the last 5 km at
4:03–4:11 /km (6:31–6:43 /mi). That's inside the regular threshold band, and faster
than the heat-adjusted band. HR climbed to 158–166 bpm, which is closer to VO2max
effort than threshold. That's mostly heat drift late in a 2-hour run, not a pacing
mistake, but the effort was harder than the pace suggests. Your form actually got
better as you sped up: cadence rose from about 172 to 190–195 spm and stride length
went from 0.85 to 1.27 m. There was no late shortening or fade, so the data shows
nothing that points to the left hamstring or glute.

**Sequencing:** you ran a 2-hour progression run, rested about 26 minutes, then
jogged a 7-minute cool-down. The long run was 7 km (4.3 mi) longer than planned and
added a threshold finish that wasn't on the schedule. It was really closer to the
26 km (16 mi) Sunday of peak week. It was a very strong run the day after
yesterday's tune-up stopped at 2.5 km, and it shows your threshold fitness is where
it needs to be four weeks out. It was also a hard load for a 27 °C (81 °F)
afternoon. Rehydrate with electrolytes tonight, and next time start hot long runs
in the cool morning.
