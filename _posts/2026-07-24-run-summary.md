### Classification rationale

This is a long run built as a staircase: 5:55/km opening, then four clean downward
steps (5:39 → 5:29 → 5:18 → ~4:55) finishing with three kilometres at 4:50–4:51/km,
then a cooldown kilometre. Total swing from first to fastest km is 65 s/km. That
structure — steadily descending pace with the quickest work in the final third — is
a **progression long run**, not a steady easy run. At 18 km it is also the longest
session of the current block.

### Notes

- **Aerobic efficiency here is genuinely impressive.** 4:50/km at 140 bpm average is
  a very strong pace-to-heart-rate ratio. HR never exceeded 144 for the whole 18 km,
  and the fastest kilometres cost only ~10 bpm more than the opening jog pace.
- **Kms 10–12 are the standout.** Pace dropped to 5:17–5:20 while HR stayed pinned at
  131–133 — pace improving with no cardiac cost at all. That is aerobic fitness
  reasserting itself.
- **Cadence responded correctly.** 169 spm at the start climbing to 180–181 spm over
  the fast kilometres, with stride length only moving 0.98 → 1.14 m. Speed came
  mostly from turnover rather than overstriding, which is the mechanically safer way
  to find pace.
- **Well-judged opening.** First three kilometres at 5:51–5:55 with HR in the 110s.
  Patient starts like this are exactly right, especially post-collapse.
- **Flat course, so the progression was intentional.** Only 37 m of gain across 18 km
  means the pace changes were driven by effort, not terrain.

### Compliance check

⚠️ **Partially outside current guidance.** Standing medical advice after the
2026-06-06 collapse is *easy effort only — no tempo, threshold, intervals, or
progression finishes* until cardiology clears harder work. Distance is **not** an
issue (the sub-5 km cap has expired, and 18 km is fine). The structure is:
three closing kilometres at 4:50/km is a deliberate progression finish, which is
the one thing explicitly ruled out for now.

The mitigating detail is real and worth stating: **HR peaked at 144 and averaged 129**,
so this never physiologically became a hard effort — it stayed aerobic throughout,
unlike the 5 × 1 km interval session on 2026-07-22. Judged by heart rate this was a
moderate run; judged by structure it was a workout.

**Recommendation:** keep the aerobic fitness, drop the descending structure. Run the
same 18 km flat at 5:20–5:30/km rather than finishing at 4:50, until GI and
cardiology follow-ups are done and the stress echo result is confirmed. Also keep
attention on pre-run carbohydrate and hydration — a 1,292 kcal session in July heat
is exactly the setup where the June hypoglycemia risk reappears, and refuelling
within 30–60 minutes matters more than usual after a run this long.
