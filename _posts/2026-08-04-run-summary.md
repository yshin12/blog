Monday's 21 km endurance run. Nothing flashy in the numbers, which is exactly the point.

**Run type: Easy — recovery pace applied to a general aerobic distance.** The average of
5:32/km (8:54/mi) sits squarely in the recovery band of 5:20–5:50/km (8:35–9:20/mi), well
outside the general aerobic band of 4:52–5:17/km (7:50–8:30/mi) that the plan nominally
called for. Average HR of 125 bpm is roughly 68% of max — under the 76% recovery ceiling for
the entire 85 minutes. This was a recovery run wearing a general aerobic distance, and given
yesterday it was the right trade.

### Shape of the run

**Warm-up (km 1–3):** 6:02, 5:37, 5:33 /km (9:42, 9:01, 8:56 /mi). A genuinely slow first
kilometre with HR at 107 bpm — the legs were given permission to wake up rather than being
told to.

**Body (km 4–15):** 5:22, 5:32, 5:16, 5:23, 5:33, 5:38, 5:31, 5:24, 5:21, 5:36, 5:32, 5:40
/km (8:37, 8:54, 8:28, 8:40, 8:56, 9:04, 8:53, 8:41, 8:36, 9:00, 8:54, 9:07 /mi). Twelve
kilometres inside a 24-second window on rolling terrain. HR sat between 121 and 132 bpm the
whole way with no upward drift at all — 124 bpm at km 7 and 129 bpm at km 14, after an hour
more of running.

**Close (final 0.39 km):** 5:32/km (8:54/mi) at 124 bpm. Finished at exactly the average
pace, no fade and no sprint.

### Plan adherence

Week 9 of the Pfitzinger build (2026-10-11 half) prescribed **Tuesday: general aerobic, 14 km
(9 miles)** at 4:52–5:17/km (7:50–8:30/mi). Distance came in over at 15.38 km, pace came in
under at 5:32/km. Counting this as on plan: Monday carried a 21.19 km long run that had been
displaced from Sunday, so Tuesday landed on legs with no rest day behind them. Running the
general aerobic slot at recovery intensity is the textbook adjustment, and the extra 1.4 km
at that effort costs nothing.

The real read on this session is the flat heart rate. Twenty-four hours after a 21 km run
finishing at 4:38/km, holding 125 bpm for 85 minutes with zero cardiac drift says the
aerobic system absorbed Monday cleanly.

### Hamstring / glute note

No signal of trouble. Cadence held at 169–174 spm across every split, stride length stayed
between 103 and 109 cm, and there was no split where pace and HR diverged the way they do
when a tendon starts guarding. The slow, flat, low-load nature of the run is about the
friendliest thing you can give a proximal hamstring the day after a long run.

Worth naming the context though: this is now roughly 37 km across two days on a tendon that
only started complaining on 2026-07-29, following a 102 km week. PHT responds to accumulated
volume, not single sessions. Keep the isometric work in — long-hold bridges, single-leg
holds — and keep leaving the static hamstring stretching alone, since stretching compresses
the proximal tendon rather than loading it.

### What's next

Wednesday is the week's real work: **lactate threshold, 16 km (10 mi) with a 20-minute LT
interval, 4-minute jog recovery, then a 16-minute LT interval** at 4:05–4:12/km
(6:34–6:45/mi). That is a step up from last week's 18 + 14 and it is the single highest-risk
session on the calendar for the hamstring. Today's easy running was the right preparation for
it. If the sit-bone area reads 2/10 or worse on Wednesday morning, cut the second interval to
10 minutes or drop the whole thing to a 20-minute steady tempo — the aerobic benefit survives
the modification, the tendon may not survive the full version.

### Takeaway

An honest easy day. Slow start, metronomic middle, no cardiac drift, no fade, no hamstring
noise — 85 minutes of pure aerobic maintenance placed exactly where the training week needed
it. Banking discipline like this on a Tuesday is what makes Wednesday's threshold session
possible.
