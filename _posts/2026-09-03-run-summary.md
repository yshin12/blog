Three activities today in Burlington, run back to back as one session: an easy warm-up, a
10 km threshold effort, and a short cool-down jog. 17.08 km (10.61 mi) in total, on a hot and
humid afternoon — 27.2 °C (81 °F) with a 20 °C (68 °F) dew point.

**Run type: Easy / warm-up.** A proper build rather than a jog: 6:57 /km (11:11 /mi) on the
opening 400 m, easing down through 6:39 and 6:28 before settling at 5:00–5:30 /km
(8:03–8:51 /mi) for the last eight segments. Heart rate crept from 108 to 130 bpm and stopped
there — well under the 76% ceiling, exactly what a warm-up should cost. Cadence rose from 164
to 186 spm across the run, so the legs were genuinely turned over before the hard work started.

**Run type: Lactate threshold (LT) — the week's 40-minute tempo.** The nominal Pfitzinger LT
band is 4:03–4:09 /km (6:31–6:41 /mi). With temperature 27.2 °C and dew point 20 °C, the
temp + dew-point sum reaches **149 °F** — top of the 140–149 band, worth roughly 3–4.5% slower
for the same physiological cost. The heat-equivalent band is therefore **4:11–4:20 /km
(6:44–6:59 /mi)**.

### Structure

| Segment | Distance | Pace | HR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening block | 5.99 km (3.72 mi) | 4:18 /km (6:55 /mi) | 128 → 172 |
| Pause / water | 0.40 km (0.25 mi) | 4:22 /km (7:02 /mi) | dip to 148 |
| Closing block | 3.62 km (2.25 mi) | 4:41 /km (7:32 /mi) | 162 → 167 |

The opening block sat squarely inside the heat-adjusted band, and the middle of it was
metronomic — 4:11, 4:09, 4:13, 4:14, 4:10, 4:14 across consecutive 400 m segments. Heart rate
climbed steadily from 128 to 172 bpm over those same six kilometres while pace held flat, which
is textbook cardiac drift in heat rather than a pacing mistake. The brief stop around 6 km
dropped HR to 148, and the restart at a slightly softer 4:41 /km kept the effort honest without
pushing into 95% max HR territory for another twenty minutes.

Cadence is the encouraging detail: 188 spm average, rising to 192–196 spm in the late segments
*as pace slowed*. Stride length shortened smoothly from 1.29 m to 1.09 m. Fatigue was absorbed
by shortening the stride, not by losing turnover — the good failure mode.

Sequencing was correct and complete: 4.84 km of progressive warm-up, 44 minutes of continuous
threshold-effort running, 2.23 km of cool-down. Roughly 28% of the day's distance was quality
work, and the two bookends account for the rest — that is the right ratio for a tempo day, and
the warm-up in particular is what protects the hamstring from a cold start at 4:10 /km.

Cumulative load lands in the moderate-hard range: 1,319 kcal, and about 26 minutes spent above
160 bpm. The average HR across the whole day works out near 145 bpm, which for 86 minutes of
running in 81 °F heat is a solid but not excessive stimulus.

## Plan note

Thursday of Pfitzinger week 5 (Aug 31 – Sep 6, 97 km target) prescribes **endurance — 19 km
(11.8 mi)**. What was actually run is **Wednesday's LT session shifted one day** — 16 km with a
40-minute tempo — after Wednesday was taken as an 8.04 km recovery jog. Judged against the
swapped intent, adherence is strong: 17.08 km against 16 km prescribed, and 44:28 of tempo
running against a 40-minute target, with the first 6 km inside the heat-adjusted LT band. The
displaced 19 km endurance run is the cost of the swap, and it is a fair trade — getting the
week's anchor workout done matters more than the volume number.

The remaining week stands: Friday recovery 8 km (5.0 mi), Saturday general aerobic 14 km
(8.7 mi) with 10 × 100 m strides, Sunday progression long run 26 km (16.1 mi) finishing with
5 km at LT pace. Five weeks to October 11.

On the left hamstring/glute, this was the highest tendon-load session in about two weeks —
44 minutes at 1.17 m stride length with real hip extension force. The data shows nothing
alarming: no split anomalies, no collapse in cadence, no sign of a protective compensation.
Isometric holds tonight rather than stretching, and if the sit-bone area is sore on waking,
keep Friday very easy and consider dropping the LT finish from Sunday's long run.
