### How it unfolded

The opening 3.2 km (2.0 mi) was a textbook recovery progression — 5:57 /km (9:34 /mi) for the first
quarter mile, easing down through 5:44, 5:40, 5:34 to 5:30 /km (8:51 /mi), then holding 5:33–5:37 /km
(8:56–9:02 /mi) to the end of the steady portion. Heart rate sat at 107 bpm on the first split and
never rose above 121 bpm for the whole easy block — comfortably inside the recovery ceiling of 76%
max. Cadence built naturally from 160 to 178 spm as the legs woke up.

Then four strides, each roughly 100 m with a jog float between:

| Stride | Pace | Max cadence | Peak HR |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3:28 /km (5:35 /mi) | 203 spm | 123 bpm |
| 2 | 3:43 /km (5:59 /mi) | 190 spm | 148 bpm |
| 3 | 3:32 /km (5:41 /mi) | 199 spm | 141 bpm |
| 4 | 3:25 /km (5:30 /mi) | 198 spm | 152 bpm |

Three of the four landed inside the speed band of 3:20–3:37 /km (5:22–5:49 /mi), with stride 2 the
only slightly conservative one. The last stride was the fastest — a good sign that the legs were
opening up rather than fading. Stride length peaked at 1.44 m, well above the 1.03 m easy-running
average, which is exactly what strides are for.

### Plan adherence

Wednesday of the 7-weeks-to-go week (Aug 17–23) called for **lactate threshold — 16 km (10 mi) with a
22 min LT interval, 4 min jog, then an 18 min LT interval** at 4:03–4:09 /km (6:31–6:41 /mi). What
actually happened was a 4.04 km (2.51 mi) recovery jog with strides — a substantial step down in
both volume and intensity. That is not a failure; a 22 + 18 min threshold double is one of the
hardest sessions in the whole build, and with the left hamstring/glute niggle still on the watch
list, an easy day with light speed work is a sensible substitution. The strides preserve neuromuscular
quality without loading the proximal hamstring the way sustained threshold work would.

Week to date sits at 19.3 km (12.0 mi) across Tue–Wed against an 89 km (55 mi) plan week, so the
threshold session is worth rescheduling rather than dropping. Sliding it to Thursday and pushing the
18 km (11 mi) endurance run back a day would keep Sunday's 23 km (14.3 mi) progression long run
intact — that is the week's real anchor session, with its last 5 km (3.1 mi) at LT pace.

### Notes

Heart rate behaviour was clean throughout: fast recovery between strides, no drift on the easy
portion, peak of 153 bpm on the final pickup and nothing alarming. Cadence in the 170s on easy
running and near 200 spm on the strides is right where it should be. Short, controlled, and
restorative — a fine reset day before the harder work resumes.
