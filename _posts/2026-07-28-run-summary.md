### Analysis

A tidy, well-controlled medium-long run. After a 5:35 opening kilometre the pace settled almost
immediately into a 5:15–5:19 /km groove and held it for seven straight kilometres — km 2 through
km 8 all sit inside a four-second band. That is unusually flat pacing for a 14 km effort on a
route with only 28 m of total climb.

The heart rate response is the encouraging part. HR rose from 117 bpm to about 133 bpm over the
first four kilometres and then effectively plateaued: km 4 at 133, km 7 at 133, km 8 at 134,
finishing at 136. Across roughly 70 minutes of running that is minimal cardiac drift, and the
session ceiling of 144 bpm stayed comfortably below any threshold territory. Despite averaging
37 seconds per kilometre quicker than yesterday's recovery run, average HR only rose 12 beats —
a good sign that aerobic fitness is re-consolidating.

Cadence tells a clear story of the second half. It climbed steadily from 170 spm early to 183–186
spm over the closing kilometres, while stride length actually *shortened* from 1.10 m to 1.02 m.
The mildly quicker splits at km 9–13 came from faster turnover rather than longer, more forceful
strides — a lower-stress way to pick up pace, and easier on the posterior chain.

### Effort & Medical Note

Current guidance remains **easy effort only** until harder work is formally cleared, and this run
stays within that boundary. There was no interval, tempo or threshold segment, no progression
finish, and the 144 bpm maximum is squarely aerobic. The 5:15 /km average is quicker than the past
week's norm, but the HR data — not the pace — is the relevant test, and it reads easy throughout.

The item worth watching is accumulated load rather than intensity. Today is the **eighth
consecutive running day**, following an 85 km week that was capped by a 22 km long run. Even at
easy effort, eight unbroken days is meaningful stress while post-June recovery capacity is still
the limiting factor. A genuine rest day or a short shakeout in the next 24–48 hours would be the
sensible call. Keep hydration and pre-run carbohydrate deliberate in the July heat — the
hypoglycaemia and dehydration lessons from June still apply.
