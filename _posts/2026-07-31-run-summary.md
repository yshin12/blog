## Endurance Progression Run — 19.00 km (11.81 mi)

The week's long-ish midweek run, and one that got faster almost every kilometre.

**Run type: Endurance / medium-long run (MLR), run as a progression.** The average of
5:01/km (8:05/mi) sits squarely inside the Pfitzinger endurance band of 4:39–5:05/km
(7:30–8:10/mi), and the shape of the run is the textbook version of that session: start
around 20% slower than goal-race pace and finish around 10% slower. Average HR of 141 bpm
is at the top of the 70–81% endurance range, and with a max of only 158 there was never a
drift into threshold territory.

### Split pattern

Kilometre splits: 5:40, 5:24, 5:09, 5:11, 5:08, 5:01, 5:00, 4:56, 4:59, 4:58, 4:48, 4:48,
4:47, 4:43, 4:46, 4:41, 4:43, 4:48, then a 6:00 cooldown kilometre.

That is a 59-second drop from the opening kilometre to the fastest one, achieved in small
steps rather than a single surge. The first 5 km averaged 5:18/km (8:32/mi); the last five
running kilometres averaged 4:44/km (7:37/mi) — a genuine negative split with the closing
block right at the fast end of endurance pace and still comfortably short of threshold
(4:05–4:12/km, 6:34–6:45/mi).

Heart rate tells the same controlled story: 115 bpm in the opening kilometre rising steadily
to 155 by the end. Roughly 40 bpm of drift across 18 kilometres while the pace dropped a
minute per kilometre is a strong aerobic signal — the cost of the extra speed was paid
gradually, not all at once.

Cadence climbed from 168 to a stable 182–188 spm and stride length went from 102 cm to
114 cm, meaning the extra speed came from both turnover and stride opening rather than
overstriding alone.

### Plan adherence

The Pfitzinger week (10 weeks to the 2026-10-11 half) had Thursday down as endurance 18 km
(11 mi) and Friday as rest or cross-training. Those two days were effectively swapped: a
6.44 km (4.00 mi) recovery jog on Thursday, the endurance run today. That is a fine
adjacent-day swap, and at 19.00 km the distance slightly exceeds the 18 km prescription.

### Hamstring / glute note

Nothing here suggests the minor left hamstring/glute niggle was aggravated. Splits improved
monotonically with no mid-run breakdown, cadence stayed high and even throughout, and stride
length grew smoothly rather than jumping. Progressive running on a mostly flat route
(46 m of gain) is one of the safer ways to load that tendon, since the fastest work happens
when tissue is warm rather than cold.

Keep the loading isometric — long-hold bridges and single-leg isometric holds — rather than
stretching the hamstring, which compresses the proximal tendon. If the sit-bone area reads
2/10 or feels stiff tomorrow morning, take the speed out of Saturday's hill-and-strides
session and keep it flat and aerobic.

### Takeaway

The best-executed run of the week. Progression runs like this one build exactly the fatigue
resistance a half marathon rewards, and finishing at 4:41/km (7:32/mi) off an easy start,
with heart rate still under control, is a strong indicator that the aerobic base is coming
back after the summer's interruption.
