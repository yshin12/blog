### Takeaways

- **This was truly easy.** HR averaged 120 and never went above 136 before the strides. Running 5:05–5:20 /km at HR in the mid-120s shows the aerobic engine is in good shape.
- **The long warm-up helped the hamstring.** Three slow kilometres warmed the proximal hamstring before normal-stride running. Cadence and stride length then rose steadily, with no sign of guarding.
- **The strides were a little too quick.** Three of the five were near-sprint (about 3:05–3:11 /km). While the tendon is on watch, the target is 3:20–3:35 /km, or about 21–22 s per 100 m.
