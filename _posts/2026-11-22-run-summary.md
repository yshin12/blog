Two runs on Sunday, 2026-11-22, in Burlington. The weather was cool and saturated: 6–8 °C, 100% humidity, light NE wind of 6–8 km/h. The route was mostly flat. An easy warm-up jog started at 13:39. After a 14-minute break, the main progression long run started at 14:16.

## 1. Warm-up Jog (13:39)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Distance | 3.43 km |
| Time | 23:06 (moving 23:04) |
| Avg pace | 6:43 /km |
| Best pace | 6:05 /km |
| Avg / max HR | 104 / 113 bpm |
| Cadence | 170 spm (max 178) |
| Stride length | 0.87 m |
| Elevation | +13 / −8 m |
| Calories | 246 kcal |

**Run type: Easy (warm-up).** The pace was slower than the recovery band (5:25–5:55 /km), and HR stayed at about 59% of max. Its only job was to loosen up in the cold before the main run. The kilometres were 6:58, 6:42 and 6:33, then 6:33 for the last 0.43 km. Each split was a little faster and HR rose only from 100 to 108.

## 2. Progression Long Run (14:16)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Distance | 21.12 km |
| Time | 1:39:07 (moving 1:39:03) |
| Avg pace | 4:41 /km |
| Best pace | 4:01 /km |
| Avg / max HR | 144 / 169 bpm |
| Cadence | 185 spm (max 201) |
| Stride length | 1.14 m |
| Elevation | +58 / −51 m |
| Calories | 1,497 kcal |

**Run type: LR (progression), with a long marathon-pace/LT finish.** The run had three steps:

| Km | Pace | Avg HR | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | 5:19–5:21 | 118–124 | Easy / GA |
| 5–7 | 5:05 → 4:59 → 4:47 | 131–140 | Building through GA |
| 8–10 | 4:36, 4:36, 4:31 | 145–150 | Bottom of the LT HR band |
| 11–15 | 4:24–4:28 | 146–155 | LT HR band |
| 16–21.1 | 4:21–4:27 | 152–166 | Top of the LT band and above |

The first half averaged about 4:59 /km and the second half about 4:27 /km. Km 11–21 all fell within 7 s/km (4:21–4:28), close to the 3:08 marathon-PB pace, and the run ended with no fade. Cadence rose from 172 to 190–195 spm and stride length from 1.04 to 1.14–1.19 m, so form got better as the run went on. The cost was heart-rate drift. Km 11–15 averaged 4:27 at 151 bpm, while km 16–21 averaged 4:24 at 160 bpm, with the last 3 km at 164–166. About 63% of the run was at LT heart rate or higher. That makes it a quality session that happened to be long, not an endurance run.

The order made sense. The very easy jog and short break meant the main run started at only 118 bpm, and all the hard running came at the end. This was the **longest training day since the October 11 half marathon** and about 31% of a **79.1 km week** (Nov 16–22). That is the biggest week since the September peak and +53% on the week before.

## Notes

- **Plan:** the working plan was an easy 12–14 km. The run was about 10 km longer than that, and the 14 km fast finish turned it into a third hard session this week, after Tuesday's LT and Thursday's steady 3 km. It went very well, but it needs recovery to match.
- **Hamstring:** this was the biggest tendon load since the Nov 8 flare. Stride and cadence peaked late with no sign of guarding. Monday morning's check decides the next run. At 0/10, rest or run 6 km at most at recovery pace. At 1/10, rest. At 2/10 or more, go back to the flare protocol.
- **Next time:** cap the fast finish at about 155–158 bpm or keep it to 8–10 km. Take 2 gels on runs this long and fast.
