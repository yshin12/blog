**Run type: Endurance / long run (LR) with a progression finish.** The average of 5:09/km
(8:18/mi) sits just outside the endurance band of 4:39–5:05/km (7:30–8:10/mi), but the
average is dragged up by a deliberately slow opening — the back half of the run is squarely
inside the band and the closing kilometres are well under it. Average HR of 145 bpm is about
80% of max, textbook endurance territory.

### Shape of the run

**Opening (km 1–3):** 5:41, 5:28, 5:14 /km (9:08, 8:47, 8:26 /mi). A proper easing-in, with
HR starting at 119 bpm and cadence climbing from 170 to 178 spm as the legs woke up.

**Cruise (km 4–13):** 5:08, 5:04, 5:16, 5:14, 5:13, 5:09, 5:14, 5:15, 5:11, 5:13 /km
(8:15, 8:09, 8:28, 8:25, 8:24, 8:18, 8:25, 8:27, 8:20, 8:23 /mi). Ten kilometres inside a
12-second window — an impressively flat stretch of pacing on a course with rolling terrain.
HR drifted only from 140 to 147 bpm across the block.

**Build (km 14–19):** 5:08, 5:05, 5:03, 5:02, 5:00, 5:09 /km (8:15, 8:10, 8:08, 8:06, 8:03,
8:18 /mi). Effort steps up gently, HR 149 → 154 bpm, stride length stretching from 108 cm to
113 cm without cadence dropping.

**Finish (km 20–21.2):** 4:54, 4:40, then 4:38 /km for the last 190 m (7:53, 7:30, 7:27 /mi).
Cadence jumped to 186 and then 191 spm, stride length to 112 cm, HR peaking at 163–164 bpm.
That is the fastest running of the day arriving at the very end of a 21 km run — a strong
finish, not a fade.

### Plan adherence

Week 10 of the Pfitzinger build (2026-10-11 half) prescribed **Sunday: endurance, 21 km
(13 miles)**. Sunday was taken as rest and the session moved to Monday, whose slot was "rest
or cross-training". A clean one-day swap, and the distance landed at 21.19 km — within 200 m
of prescription.

Executed exactly as Pfitzinger describes an endurance run should be: start around 20% slower
than 15K–HM pace and finish about 10% slower. The opening kilometre at 5:41/km and the
closing pair at 4:54 and 4:40/km bracket that intent almost perfectly. Week 10 closes at
**102.11 km (63.45 mi)** against a 78 km target — a substantial overshoot, driven by the
extra morning shakeouts earlier in the week.

### Hamstring / glute note

The best possible news here. This was the longest run since the left hamstring/glute niggle
appeared on 2026-07-29, and the leg answered by producing the fastest kilometres of the run
in the final two. A tendon that is unhappy does the opposite — cadence falls, stride
shortens, and the closing splits sag. Instead cadence rose from 180 to 191 spm and stride
length held at 112 cm. There is no asymmetry signal anywhere in the data.

The caveat is cumulative: 102 km in a week with a tendon on watch is a lot of repetitions,
and proximal hamstring tendinopathy responds to accumulated volume rather than any single
session. Check the sit-bone area tomorrow morning. Keep the isometric work going — long-hold
bridges and single-leg holds — and continue to leave the hamstring stretching alone, since
stretching compresses the proximal tendon rather than loading it.

### What's next

Week 9 opens with Tuesday's **general aerobic 14 km (8.7 mi)** at 4:52–5:17/km
(7:50–8:30/mi), then Wednesday's step up to a **16 km (10 mi) lactate threshold session, 20
min plus 16 min at 4:05–4:12/km (6:34–6:45/mi)**. Given today's 21 km landed on what should
have been a rest day, the sensible adjustment is to run Tuesday at the slow end of the
general aerobic band — nearer 5:15/km (8:27/mi) — and skip any bonus shakeout. Wednesday is
the week's real work, and it deserves fresh legs.

### Takeaway

A very good long run. Ten kilometres of metronomic cruising followed by a genuine six
kilometre build and a sub-4:40/km finish is exactly the aerobic quality this phase is meant
to develop, and the hamstring came through it without a complaint. The one thing to watch is
the ledger: the week finished 24 km over plan, so the next few days should be honest easy
running rather than more accidental volume.
