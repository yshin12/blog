**Run type: General Aerobic + Speed.** Pfitzinger HM Schedule 2, weeks-to-goal 8 (Recovery week),

### The strides

Ten reps, average 29.4 s, fastest 28.8 and slowest 29.8 — a **one-second spread across the whole
session**, and set 2 (29.5 s average) essentially identical to set 1 (29.2 s). That is about as
even as strides get, and the second set showing no decay is the number that matters. At 19.6 s per
100 m the reps ran 3:16 /km (5:15 /mi), a shade quicker than the speed band of 3:20–3:37 /km
(5:22–5:49 /mi) — fast, but over 150 m that is fine and it never turned into a sprint.

Mechanics tell the same story. Cadence lifted from 175 spm on the warmup laps to about 195 spm in
the reps, and stride length from 1.09 m to roughly 1.43 m. The speed came from turnover *and*
extension together, which is exactly what strides are for: reminding the legs how to move fast
without the aerobic cost of a real workout.

Heart rate behaved as it should. The warmup sat at 105–131 bpm, reps peaked at 163 with a session
max of 177 on the jog after rep 7, and the average across the full 51 minutes was only 137 bpm.
Short reps with generous recovery buy neuromuscular work at almost no metabolic price — the point
of putting speed inside a recovery week.

### Context

Week 8-to-goal is the recovery week with a 75 km (47 mi) target. Today puts the week at
**78.9 km (49.1 mi) through Saturday**, already past the target with Sunday's 18 km (11 mi)
endurance run still on the card. Running that in full would land the week near 97 km (60 mi) — the
territory of the planned peak week three weeks from now, in a week whose job was absorption.
Trimming Sunday to 14–16 km (9–10 mi) at the easy end of the endurance band, 5:10–5:24 /km
(8:19–8:41 /mi), keeps the long-run habit without paying for it on Wednesday, when the real work
returns: 16 km with 22 min and 18 min at LT pace, 4:03–4:09 /km (6:31–6:41 /mi).

For the left hamstring/glute, this was the week's first genuine speed exposure and the data reads
clean. Rep times held flat to the last one, the second set matched the first, and cadence and
stride length stayed level rep to rep — no shortening, no guarding, no bailing out of the fast
segments. A flat track with no hills and full 250 m jog recoveries was the right venue for it. The
honest test is tomorrow morning: if the sit-bone area is quiet on waking, Wednesday's threshold
block stays as written. If it is stiff or tender at 2/10 or more, the LT minutes come down first
and the endurance volume stays.
