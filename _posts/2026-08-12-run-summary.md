One activity today: "Burlington Running," a 16.01 km (9.95 mi) general aerobic run starting at
19:35. This was the biggest ask of the recovery week, and it was executed the way a GA run is
supposed to look — a genuinely slow first kilometre, a long patient ramp, and eight closing
kilometres locked into a narrow pace band with heart rate barely moving.

**Run type: General aerobic (AR).** Pfitzinger HM Schedule 2, weeks-to-goal 8 (Recovery week),
Wednesday — prescribed "General aerobic 16 km (10 mi)." Actual: 16.01 km at 5:01 /km
(8:04 /mi), which sits inside the general aerobic band of 4:54–5:20 /km (7:53–8:35 /mi). Distance
to the metre, intensity in the right tier — this is as close to a clean hit as the plan gets.

The shape here is the story. The first 8 km averaged 5:09 /km (8:17 /mi) and the second 8 km
averaged 4:53 /km (7:51 /mi) — a 16 s/km (26 s/mi) negative split, and the back half never
wandered more than five seconds either side of 4:53 /km (7:51 /mi). Heart rate paid for it
modestly: 132 bpm average over the first half against 144 bpm over the second, and once the pace
settled at km 8 the HR simply plateaued in the 142–146 range for forty minutes rather than
climbing. At roughly 74% of max on average and 78% on the closing kilometres, this stayed
comfortably aerobic despite finishing near the fast edge of the GA band.

Cadence averaged 175 spm and stride length grew from 1.05 m to 1.17 m across the run, so once
again the extra speed came from lengthening rather than from thrashing turnover — the signature of
legs that felt good rather than legs being pushed.

### Context

Week 8-to-goal is the recovery week (75 km / 47 mi target) and this was its largest session. It
follows Monday's deferred 23 km progression long run and Tuesday's genuinely easy 10.16 km, so
three days in the legs already and no sign of accumulated fatigue in the numbers — if anything the
closing pace was quicker than a recovery week strictly demands. Worth keeping honest: the week is
running ahead of plan on volume, and Saturday is the natural place to give some back.

Thursday is the first speed touch of the week — 14 km (9 mi) general aerobic with 2 sets of
5 × 150 m strides at 3:20–3:37 /km (5:22–5:49 /mi), jogging 250 m between reps and 4 minutes
between sets.

The left hamstring/glute area came through cleanly again. Sixteen kilometres on flat ground with a
steadily lengthening stride and no late pace decay is a reassuring signal — nothing in the splits
suggests the tendon was protesting. Thursday's strides are the item to watch, since short fast
reps load that region harder than any steady running does; dropping to a single set is the sensible
response if anything speaks up in the first five.
