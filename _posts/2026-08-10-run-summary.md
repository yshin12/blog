**Run type: Progression Long Run (LR).** Pfitzinger HM Schedule 2, weeks-to-goal 9, Sunday —
"Progression long run 23 km (14 mi); increase effort steadily throughout, last 3 km (2 mi) at LT
pace." Distance was hit exactly, and the progression itself was textbook.

### How the effort built

| Segment | Distance | Avg pace | Avg HR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening (km 1–5) | 5 km (3.11 mi) | 6:41 /km (10:45 /mi) | 110 |
| Middle (km 6–12) | 7 km (4.35 mi) | 5:26 /km (8:44 /mi) | 125 |
| Closing (km 13–23) | 11 km (6.84 mi) | 4:49 /km (7:45 /mi) | 147 |
| **Last 3 km** | 3 km (1.86 mi) | **4:35 /km (7:22 /mi)** | 156 |

The first 12 km averaged 5:59 /km (9:38 /mi) and the last 11 km averaged 4:49 /km (7:45 /mi) — a
negative split of about 70 s/km, with no single kilometre breaking the downward trend after km 11.
Heart rate tracked it smoothly from 102 bpm to a peak of 161 bpm (~89% max) on km 22, and cadence
climbed 164 → 189 spm while stride length stretched 0.85 m → 1.18 m. That is the right way to get
faster late in a long run: turnover and stride opening together, rather than overstriding.

The one gap versus the prescription is the closing intensity. The final 3 km at 4:35 /km
(7:22 /mi) sits roughly 26 s/km above the 4:03–4:09 /km (6:31–6:41 /mi) LT band — strong endurance
running, but not quite threshold. Coming at the end of a two-hour run on tired legs, and with the
opening 5 km run very conservatively at 6:41 /km, that is an understandable trade: the aerobic
volume was banked, the LT stimulus was only partial.

### Context

This run opens the recovery week (weeks-to-goal 8, 75 km target), even though it belongs to the
previous week in intent. With the deferred long run now on the books, the rest of the week is all
general aerobic and strides — no hard workouts on the plan — which is well-timed after a 90 km
week plus this 23 km effort. The left hamstring/glute area held up through a session that finished
at 4:31 /km, which is a genuinely reassuring signal.
