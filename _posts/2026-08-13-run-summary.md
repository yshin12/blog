One activity today: "9W Thu Recovery 5k," a 5.04 km (3.13 mi) recovery jog starting at 20:07.
After three consecutive substantial days to open the recovery week, this was a short, genuinely
easy shakeout — low heart rate, gentle turnover, and a quiet upward drift in pace that never
turned into effort.

**Run type: Recovery.** Pfitzinger HM Schedule 2, weeks-to-goal 8 (Recovery week), Thursday —
prescribed "General aerobic 14 km (9 mi) with 2 sets of 5 × 150 m strides." Actual: 5.04 km at
5:47 /km (9:18 /mi), no strides. That is a substantial trim, and the run itself sat squarely in
the recovery band of 5:25–5:55 /km (8:45–9:30 /mi) rather than the general aerobic band.

The profile is exactly what a recovery run should look like: a slow opening kilometre, then a
31 s/km (59 s/mi) gentle progression across five kilometres with heart rate rising only from 106
to 123 bpm. Average HR of 114 is roughly 62% of max — well under the 76% recovery ceiling and the
lowest of any session this week by a wide margin. Cadence held at 169 spm with stride length near
1.02 m, both modest, which is the correct signature for a day whose job is circulation rather than
stimulus.

### Context

Week 8-to-goal is the recovery week (75 km / 47 mi target), and the first three days already
banked 23.06 km, 10.16 km and 16.01 km. Adding today, the week stands at **54.27 km (33.7 mi)
through Thursday**. With Saturday's 14 km and Sunday's 18 km still to come, the week was tracking
to overshoot its target — so trading a 14 km general aerobic session for a 5 km jog pulls the
recovery week back toward being an actual recovery week. Judged against the intent of the week
rather than the letter of the Thursday line, this was a sound call.

The one item genuinely deferred is the speed touch. Saturday is already a general-aerobic-plus-speed
day, so the 150 m strides can simply move there — a single set of 5 × 150 m at 3:20–3:37 /km
(5:22–5:49 /mi) is enough to keep the neuromuscular thread alive without stacking two stride
sessions into the back half of the week.

The left hamstring/glute area had the easiest day it has had all week. Low cadence, short stride,
no fast reps and no hills means essentially zero provocative load on that tendon, which makes today
a useful reset before Saturday's strides — the one session in the coming week most likely to test
it. If the region feels quiet through Friday's rest day, the full set is fair game; if anything
speaks up in the first two reps, stopping at three is the sensible answer.
