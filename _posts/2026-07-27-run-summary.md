### Analysis

This is what the day after a 22 km long run should look like. The opening kilometre at 6:07 /km
with HR at 110 bpm shows the run started deliberately slow, and the middle block (km 4–8) sat in
an almost metronomic 5:53–5:56 /km window with HR pinned between 120 and 123 bpm. Five
consecutive kilometres inside a three-second pace band and a three-beat HR band is impressive
control on tired legs.

The cardiac numbers are the headline: 121 bpm average and a session max of only 135 bpm. There is
essentially no cardiac drift across 65 minutes — HR at km 8 (122) is identical to HR at km 5
(123), despite the accumulated fatigue of a seven-day running streak. Stride length opened
gently from 0.94 m to 1.07 m while cadence held in a tight 169–178 spm band, so the modest
late-run quickening came from relaxation rather than effort.

The final three kilometres (5:46, 5:39, 5:28) do drift downward, but this looks like legs loosening
up rather than a deliberate push — HR only nudged to 130 bpm at the fastest kilometre, still
comfortably inside easy territory.

### Effort & Medical Note

Current cardiology guidance is **easy effort only** until harder work is formally cleared, and
this run is fully compliant. A 135 bpm maximum is the lowest ceiling in the past week, there is no
tempo or threshold segment, and the mild negative split stayed well short of a progression finish.
This is exactly the kind of session to keep repeating while awaiting the GI and cardiology
follow-ups.

One thing worth watching: today closes a **seven-consecutive-day** block totalling 96 km, capped
by yesterday's 22 km. Even at easy effort that is a lot of unbroken load. A genuine rest day or a
short shakeout in the next day or two would be the sensible move — particularly given that
recovery capacity is still the limiting factor post-June. Continue prioritising hydration and
pre-run carbohydrate in the July heat.
