### Why it counts as Recovery

- **HR stayed very low:** average 122 bpm, about 67% of max. The peak of 138 only touched the 76% recovery ceiling right at the end.
- **Average pace sat in the band:** 5:28 /km is inside the 5:25–5:55 /km recovery range. The first 3 km were a very relaxed 6:00–6:30 /km warm-up.
- **The finish drifted faster:** km 7–10 went 5:07 → 4:49 /km, which is general aerobic pace. HR still only rose to 131–133 bpm while the pace dropped by about 1:40 /km over the run. That flat HR response is a good sign that the legs are coming back four days after the race.
- **Form was good:** cadence settled at 180–185 spm, and stride length grew smoothly from 0.88 m to 1.13 m as the pace rose. There was nothing in the splits to suggest guarding the left hamstring/glute.

### Plan adherence

The recovery week (Oct 12–18) prescribed a **recovery run of 10 km** for Thursday. Youngki ran 10.17 km, so the distance was right on target and the effort was at recovery level overall. The only point to note is the last 3–4 km, which were quicker than a strict recovery run needs to be. In a recovery week it is better to keep the whole run at 5:25 /km or slower and save faster running for Saturday's strides.
