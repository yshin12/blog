These are the conditions the race build has been waiting for, and the low heart rate at every pace today reflects it.

A textbook progression shape: five miles well below recovery pace (heart rate dropping to 104 by mile 4), then a 1:39 drop into mile 6 and a steady squeeze all the way to a closing 7:39 mile. The last three miles came at 8:16, 8:01 and 7:39 /mi for 118, 129 and 139 bpm — endurance pace on a heart rate that would qualify as recovery on most days. Cadence tracked the effort honestly too, from 166 spm in the opening mile to 183 spm at the finish.

### Plan adherence

Prescribed (Pfitzinger HM Schedule 2, 3 weeks to goal, Saturday): **General aerobic — 10 mi (16 km)**.
Actual: **10.05 mi (16.18 km)** at 9:36 /mi (5:58 /km).

Distance is exactly on plan. The average pace sits below the general aerobic band of 8:03–9:07 /mi (5:00–5:40 /km), but that average is misleading — the first half was deliberately jogged and the back half was run at 8:16 down to 7:39 /mi, i.e. inside and then past the GA band and into endurance territory. Average HR of 116 is roughly 66 % of max, so the aerobic cost of the whole session was low. On the day after a recovery run and two days after 5 × 1,200 m, a slow-start progression is a sensible way to bank peak-week mileage without adding fatigue before tomorrow's long run.
