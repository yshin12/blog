### Notes

Metronomic pacing — every lap in block 1 landed inside a 1.8-second window (1:42.1–1:43.9), which is about as even as a track workout gets. Block 2 held the same rhythm for six laps before drifting ~3 seconds per lap over the final three, a normal and honest fade at the end of 8 km of threshold work in warm conditions.

The pace itself sits right at half-marathon effort (HM PB 1:28:39 = 4:12/km), so this was a true LT stimulus rather than a disguised interval session. Cadence tells the same story: 176 spm on the warm-up rising to 187–190 spm through both blocks, with stride length holding near 1.21 m instead of collapsing — form stayed organized under fatigue.

Cardiac drift was modest: block 1 averaged 162 bpm, block 2 averaged 165 bpm for slightly slower pace, a ~3 bpm decoupling across the session. The 170 bpm peak came in the closing laps and is close to age-predicted max (~171), so this was a genuinely hard top end — appropriate for a first quality day back, but not a session to repeat twice in a week.

The progressive warm-up (5:37 down to 4:51) and the stride set before the first hard lap were well judged, especially with the left hamstring/glute niggle in play: the tendon went into threshold pace already warm and already cycling through fast turnover. Two sustained tempo blocks are also a friendlier choice for that tissue than short reps with hard accelerations. Worth checking how the hamstring feels tomorrow morning — an easy day is the plan either way.
